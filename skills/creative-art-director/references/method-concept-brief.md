# Method: Concept and brief (the big idea and the one-page brief)

## 1. When to read this
"What is the idea", positioning, a concept set, "write the brief for the artist and the video team", "a rival's ad is doing well, copy it".

## 2. Steps
1. **Frame:** the goal (installs, retention, a new look), the game, the audience, the use, the limits (platforms, markets, deadline, money). With no goal or no audience, return `needs_answers` (at most 3 questions, each with a suggested default) and write no brief yet.
2. **Gather:** read the game description, the style or brand guide, past work, lessons and other roles' findings the brief passes on. Label each fact `verified`, `inferred` or `unknown`. A feature marked "planned" is not in the game.
3. **Position:** player, promise, rival, proof. Run the competitor swap test: if a rival's name fits the idea just as well, there is no idea yet.
4. **Explore** three to five ideas that really differ (not one idea in five colours). Cut any idea the real game cannot honestly show.
5. **Rank** them against goal, audience, cost to make and risk; say what each gives up. Return the choice as `needs_approval` with your recommendation. Do not pick alone.
6. **Brief:** after the owner's choice, write the one-page brief for the chosen idea.

## 3. Brand guide wins
If the request asks for a look that conflicts with the game's brand or style guide ("dark and gritty" against "bright and friendly"), keep the guide, flag the conflict, and ask one question with a default (default: follow the guide).

## 4. "Copy the rival"
Take the lesson (the player need, the type of hook), not the look, the characters, the music or the slogan. Flag where the copyright or trademark line may be, "not legal advice; a lawyer decides" (see `honest-ads-and-rights.md`).

## 5. Skeletons (no separate template files exist)
Concept set: `concept | one-line idea | player and promise | proof in the real game | cost to make | risk | what it gives up | rank`

Brief (one page):
- Goal and how success will be judged
- Audience
- The one message
- Proof (from the real game)
- Tone
- References, each with where it came from (and "reference only, not to copy")
- Must show / must not show
- Formats and uses
- Done when (a short checklist)
- Open flags (rights, honesty, children)

Words in the brief are direction; the final words, script and shot list are the Writer's.

## 6. Where files go
`docs/creative/` (positioning, concept sets, briefs, risk and rights notes).

## 7. Done when
At least two really different concepts with a ranked recommendation; after the choice, a brief with must-show and must-not-show; every claim labelled; rights and honesty risks listed; and the answer says what was not checked.
