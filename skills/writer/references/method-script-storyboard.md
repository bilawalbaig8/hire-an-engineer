# Method: Script and storyboard mode (video ads, trailers, playable-ad flows)

Carried over from the Storyboard / Scriptwriter part role. Its folder `docs/storyboards/` is now `docs/writing/storyboards/`.

## 1. When to read this
A video ad or trailer script, hooks for the first seconds, a beat sheet, a shot list or storyboard notes, voice-over and captions, a store preview video, a playable-ad flow, or a variant plan.

## 2. Two sub-modes
- **Ad / Trailer:** a video script and shot list.
- **Playable flow:** screens and interaction steps: first touch, short tutorial cue, a stretch of play, the fail or win, the end card with the install button, and what happens if the player does nothing.
If unclear, ask one question with a suggested default (default: Ad / Trailer).

## 3. Steps
1. Read the brief: the game or product, the audience, the platform, the length, the idea, and what must not be shown.
2. Find what is real: list the game moments that can be shown on screen without faking them.
3. Beat sheet: hook, middle (problem or action), payoff, call to action, with seconds per beat (for example 6, 15 or 30 seconds in total).
4. At least three hook options, each with the first shot and the first words.
5. Shot list (one row per shot: what is on screen, camera, length, sound, on-screen text) and storyboard notes; add voice-over and captions with timing. A voice-over must be speakable in its time slot; if it does not fit, cut it and say what was cut and why.
6. Format notes: 9:16 first, then square and wide; safe zones where platform buttons cover the picture; captions that work with the sound off. Read each platform's live spec page for numbers; a number not read today is `unknown`.
7. Check every claim against the real game and the platform and store policy pages; mark what could mislead (claims list, `claims-and-sources.md`).
8. Variant plan: one change per variant, a name for each, and what result would count.
9. No flashing that could trigger seizures; motion in an ad is judged by the ad's goal (see `hire-an-engineer/references/motion.md`).

## 4. Where files go
`docs/writing/storyboards/`: script, shot list, storyboard sheet, caption and voice-over file, variant plan, claims list.

## 5. Lines with other roles
The idea, hook direction and beats come from the Creative & Art Director; you write the scenes and lines. The look and frames: Creative & Art Director and Artist. The cut, recorded voice, music and sound: Video & Sound Editor. Whether a variant won: Data Scientist & Analyst.

## 6. Traps
- The brief asks to show something the game does not have: flag it as misleading, use real moments only.
- "Copy this competitor's ad shot by shot": refuse the copy, write an original with the same goal.
- Paid video or voice generation, real people's likeness or voice: approval first.

## 7. Done when
A beat sheet with timings, a shot list tied to real game footage or named assets, caption and voice-over lines, a claims list with a status for each claim, and a variant plan exist; the answer says what was not checked. Never say an ad "will work".
